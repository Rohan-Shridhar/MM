Introduction to Marketing

Complete study notes covering marketing definitions, value, customer needs, market orientation, strategy, services, marketing evolution and contemporary issues.

What You Will Learn

This week introduces marketing as an organization-wide exchange and value-creation process—not simply selling or promotion. You will connect customer needs and perceived value to market orientation, STPD strategy and the 4 Ps, then apply those ideas to product positioning, service experience, marketing’s evolution and its contemporary responsibilities.

These concepts matter because organizations must understand what customers value, organize to deliver it, and consider the wider effects of their decisions. The examples and frameworks below connect strategic choices to practical delivery.

Summary — Consolidated Lecture Notes

Defining marketing and the concept of value

Selling and promotion are activities within marketing, but marketing begins earlier—with understanding customer needs—and extends beyond the transaction through delivery, satisfaction and relationship management.

Marketing as a social exchange process

A social process through which individuals and groups obtain what they need and want by creating, offering and freely exchanging valuable products and services.

Marketing as an organizational function

The American Marketing Association defines marketing as an organizational function and a set of processes for creating, communicating and delivering value to customers, and managing customer relationships for organizational benefit.

Conditions required for exchange

  • At least two parties must be involved.
  • Each party must possess something of value to the other.
  • Each party must be capable of communicating and delivering.
  • Each party must be free to accept or reject the exchange.
  • Each party must believe that dealing with the other is desirable.

Three core organizational marketing activities

  1. Creating value: Understand customer needs, define product specifications, design offerings and develop production methods. Customers may participate in co-creation from early stages.
  2. Communicating value: Inform customers about the offering, its features and the benefits it provides.
  3. Delivering value: Make the offering accessible through distribution, logistics and supply-chain management.

The concept of value

Perceived Value = Perceived Benefits − Perceived Costs

Benefits

  • Functional benefits: usefulness and performance.
  • Emotional benefits: feelings, confidence or enjoyment.
  • Symbolic benefits: identity, image or status.

Costs

  • Monetary: price paid.
  • Time: time spent searching, buying or waiting.
  • Energy: physical or mental effort.
  • Psychic: stress, uncertainty or perceived risk.
Remember: Customers choose based on perceived value, not just the lowest price. An offering becomes attractive when its perceived benefits outweigh its perceived costs.

The simple marketing system

The system connects sellers (industry) with buyers (market). Sellers provide products or services in exchange for money or other returns. Buyers also provide information and feedback, helping sellers understand needs and improve future offerings. This feedback loop is essential to customer-focused marketing.

Core concepts in marketing

Sales orientation versus market orientation

Sales-oriented approach (Product-out)Market-oriented approach (Customer-in)
Starts with the product the company has made.Starts with customer needs and market understanding.
Emphasizes selling and promotion.Emphasizes creating and delivering customer value.
Focuses on converting existing output into sales.Focuses on satisfying customers and building lasting success.

Market orientation: Sense, Share, Respond

  • Sense: Gather market intelligence about customer needs, competitors and changes in the market. This includes intelligence generation.
  • Share: Disseminate market insights across departments so the organization has a shared understanding of customers.
  • Respond: Use the information to design and deliver offerings that address declared and latent customer needs. This is responsiveness.

Need, Want and Demand

ConceptMeaningExampleMarketing role
NeedA basic, felt state of deprivation.Hunger.Identify needs.
WantThe specific form a need takes, influenced by culture and personality.Wanting a particular meal.Influence wants.
DemandA want backed by the ability and willingness to purchase.Wanting and being able to pay for that meal.Focus on actionable demand.
Easy memory aid: Need = deprivation → Want = preferred solution → Demand = want + buying power.

Product as a bundle of benefits

A product is not merely a physical object. It is a bundle of benefits that can be functional (what it does), emotional (how it makes a person feel) and symbolic (what it communicates about identity or status).

Strategic marketing process: STPD

  1. Segmentation: Divide a broad market into groups with similar needs or characteristics.
  2. Targeting: Evaluate the segments and choose which group or groups to serve.
  3. Positioning: Create a distinct and meaningful image of the offering in the target customer's mind.
  4. Differentiation: Establish meaningful ways the offering provides distinct value compared with competitors.

Tactical marketing mix: the 4 Ps

PWhat it covers
ProductFeatures, quality, design, branding and packaging.
PricePrice level, discounts, payment terms and pricing strategy.
PlaceDistribution channels, availability, coverage, inventory and logistics.
PromotionAdvertising, personal selling, sales promotion and public relations.

Strategy before tactics: Understand the market, decide whom to serve and how to position the offering, then use the 4 Ps to implement the strategy.

Case studies in marketing success and failure

Honda Activa — market-oriented product development

Customer need: A practical, easy-to-use, fuel-efficient scooter suitable for everyday urban travel and multiple family members.

Offering and value: Gearless riding, convenient starting, storage and anti-theft features, along with competitive pricing and attention to total cost of ownership.

Marketing support: Targeted promotion and an extensive service network helped make ownership practical and convenient.

Lesson: Success comes from understanding customer needs and combining useful product features with pricing, promotion and distribution that create strong perceived value.

Tata Nano — the danger of misaligned positioning

Original idea: An affordable car for the Indian market.

Problem: Positioning it as the “cheapest car” created a perception that conflicted with the aspirational and status-related meanings many customers attach to car ownership.

Some customers could prefer a second-hand vehicle if it provided greater perceived status. Functional value and a low price could not fully overcome the negative positioning.

Lesson: Positioning must align with customers' perceptions and aspirations. Low price alone does not guarantee success.

Marketing of services: the expanded mix

Services are intangible offerings, so customers often evaluate not only the outcome but also the people, process and environment through which the service is delivered. Marketing services therefore emphasizes the overall customer experience.

The IHIP characteristics of services

CharacteristicMeaningSimple example
IntangibilityA service cannot generally be touched or inspected like a physical product before purchase.A haircut or hotel stay is experienced rather than examined as a physical object beforehand.
HeterogeneityService quality can vary by employee, customer, place or occasion.A salon experience differs depending on the stylist or visit.
InseparabilityProduction and consumption often occur at the same time, with the provider involved in delivery.A haircut is delivered while the customer receives it.
PerishabilityUnused service capacity cannot be stored for later sale.An empty hotel room for a night cannot be sold again for that past night.

The 7 Ps of marketing

ElementMeaning
ProductThe service or solution offered.
PriceWhat the customer pays and the pricing conditions.
PlaceWhere and how customers access the service.
PromotionHow the service is communicated to potential customers.
PeopleEmployees and other people involved in service delivery; training, motivation and satisfaction matter.
ProcessThe activities and procedures used to deliver the service consistently.
Physical EvidenceTangible cues and surroundings that help customers judge an otherwise intangible service.

Disneyland and experience excellence

  • Positive Denial: When a request cannot be met, offer a useful alternative instead of simply saying no.
  • Imagineering: Engineering imagination into a designed experience.
  • Guestology: The systematic study of guests and their needs and experiences.

Service Marketing Triangle

  • External marketing: Making promises to customers.
  • Internal marketing: Preparing and supporting employees to deliver on promises.
  • Interactive marketing: The actual interaction between employees and customers during service delivery.
Taj Mahal Hotel case: Excellence in people, processes and physical evidence can become a powerful form of marketing. Consistently excellent service can create customer advocacy—customers who recommend the service to others.

Evolution of marketing thought and offerings

Marketing thought has evolved as organizations have changed their understanding of what drives success.

  1. Production concept: Emphasizes mass production, efficiency, availability and affordability.
  2. Product concept: Emphasizes product quality, performance and features.
  3. Selling concept: Relies on aggressive selling and promotion to move products.
  4. Marketing concept: Focuses on understanding and satisfying customer needs.
  5. Market orientation concept: Makes market intelligence and customer responsiveness an organization-wide commitment.
  6. Holistic marketing concept: Integrates the many interconnected dimensions of marketing.

Four dimensions of holistic marketing

Internal marketing

Marketing to employees and ensuring they are equipped and motivated to serve customers.

Integrated marketing

Coordinating channels and activities so the organization communicates a consistent message and delivers a coherent experience.

Relationship marketing

Building long-term, mutually beneficial relationships with customers, partners and suppliers.

Socially responsible marketing

Considering ethical, environmental and societal impacts alongside business outcomes.

Types of market offerings

  • Physical goods: Tangible items.
  • Services: Activities or benefits provided to customers.
  • Service bundles / destinations: Combined experiences such as destination marketing.
  • Ideas: Messages or causes, such as “Make in India.”
  • Organizations: Marketing an institution or organization.
  • Personalities: Marketing people or public figures.
  • Properties: Marketing property or ownership interests.

Contemporary issues in modern marketing

Marketing has evolved from a specialized department into an organization-wide philosophy. It is influential, but it also faces criticism when practices are manipulative, consume resources irresponsibly or harm society.

IssueExplanation and significance
GlobalizationReduced barriers can intensify competition, encourage standardization and create opportunities for international collaboration.
Green marketingPromotes environmentally friendly products, services and organizational behavior; helps respond to sustainability concerns, regulations and consumer expectations.
Information technologyEnables precise targeting, measurement of return on investment (ROI), e-commerce and database marketing. Marketers must keep developing digital skills.
Marketing beyond for-profit organizationsMarketing concepts are used by non-profits, political entities and social institutions as well as commercial businesses.
Relationship marketingSupports retention, loyalty and long-term profitability rather than focusing only on acquiring new customers.
Internal marketingEmployee satisfaction, training and support help employees deliver better customer service.
Marketing logisticsDistribution and supply-chain decisions help ensure products are available at the right time and place.
Stakeholder valueOrganizations are increasingly expected to create both economic and non-economic benefits for customers, employees, partners, society and other stakeholders.
Critical perspective: Marketing can create value, but it can also be criticized for manipulative practices, resource consumption and negative social effects. Responsible marketing considers these consequences rather than measuring success only through sales.

Key Takeaways

  • Marketing is the process of creating, communicating and delivering value, not merely selling or advertising.
  • Perceived value depends on benefits—functional, emotional and symbolic—minus costs such as money, time, energy and psychological effort.
  • Market orientation follows Sense → Share → Respond.
  • Need is deprivation; want is a specific preference; demand is a want backed by buying power.
  • STPD guides strategic choices; the 4 Ps implement them tactically.
  • Positioning must fit customer perceptions and aspirations; Tata Nano illustrates how the “cheapest” image can undermine appeal.
  • Services use the 7 Ps because of their IHIP characteristics: Intangibility, Heterogeneity, Inseparability and Perishability.
  • Excellent service delivery and customer experience can become powerful marketing tools.
  • Marketing evolved from production and product focus toward customer orientation and holistic, socially responsible value creation.
  • Modern marketing must account for globalization, technology, sustainability, relationships, logistics and stakeholder value.

Conclusion

Marketing is a dynamic, strategic and holistic discipline grounded in understanding customer needs, creating value and fostering relationships. Its frameworks help organizations choose customers to serve and how to serve them; its service and ethical dimensions emphasize experience and responsibility. As technology and expectations change, customer-centric adaptation and sustainable value creation remain essential.

Quiz / Practice Questions

Try each question before revealing its answer. The source options and answer explanations are retained.

1. A company focuses mainly on producing products and later attempts to sell them to customers. Another company studies customer needs first and then creates, communicates and delivers value accordingly. Which statement is correct?

Correct answer: a) First is sales; second is marketing.
Sales focuses on selling products that have already been produced, while marketing starts by understanding customer needs and then creating, communicating and delivering value to satisfy those needs.

2. A company first analyzes customers, competitors, and its own capabilities. It then selects target markets and decides how the product should be positioned. Finally, it determines the product features, pricing, distribution and promotional activities. Which sequence is correct?

Correct answer: b) Analysis → Strategy → Marketing Mix.
Marketing decisions usually follow a logical sequence: first the company analyzes the market and competitors; next it develops a strategy by selecting target markets and positioning; finally it implements the strategy through the marketing mix—product, price, place and promotion.

3. A company separates the market into different customer groups with similar needs, chooses one specific group to serve and develops a distinct image for its product in the minds of those customers. Which option shows the correct order of steps?

Correct answer: d) Segmentation → Targeting → Positioning.
The company first divides the market into groups (Segmentation), then selects the most suitable group to serve (Targeting), and finally creates a distinct image for the product in that group's mind (Positioning).

4. Although the Tata Nano was launched as an innovative low-cost car for the Indian market, it failed to achieve the expected market success over time. What was the main reason for this outcome?

Correct answer: c) It was seen as the cheapest car instead of an affordable innovation.
The Tata Nano was positioned as an affordable car, but customers largely perceived it as the “cheapest car.” This created a negative image and affected its appeal, especially among customers who wanted value and status along with affordability.

5. Honda Activa became highly successful in India by identifying an important market need among Indian consumers. Which market opportunity did Honda Activa successfully address?

Correct answer: d) Demand for a simple, fuel-efficient, easy-to-use family scooter.
The Honda Activa addressed the need for a scooter that was easy to ride, practical, fuel-efficient and suitable for everyday family use. By meeting this important customer need, it gained widespread acceptance in the Indian market.

6. A customer visits a salon and notices that the quality of service changes depending on the employee providing the service and the day of the visit. As a result, the experience is not always the same. Which unique characteristic of services is reflected in this situation?

Correct answer: c) Heterogeneity.
Heterogeneity means that service quality can vary from one employee, customer or occasion to another. In this case, the salon experience changes depending on who provides the service and when it is provided.

7. A company gradually changed its approach from mass production to improved products, then to aggressive selling, customer focus and finally to creating long-term value for all stakeholders. Which option shows the correct order of marketing evolution?

Correct answer: a) Production → Product → Selling → Marketing → Market Orientation → Holistic Marketing.
Marketing evolved from production focus to product improvement, then aggressive selling, followed by a customer-focused marketing approach. It later moved toward market orientation and finally holistic marketing, which considers long-term value for customers, the organization and other stakeholders.

8. A company distributes customer insights across different departments and then uses those insights to design products, choose target markets and launch marketing campaigns that better satisfy customer needs. Which dimension of market orientation is reflected when the company takes action based on the shared information?

Correct answer: d) Responsiveness.
Responsiveness means taking action based on customer and market information. Here the company uses shared customer insights to design products, select target markets and create campaigns that better meet customer needs.

9. A company adopts a strong market-oriented approach. Over time, it experiences higher profits, increased sales growth and improved market share. Which consequence of market orientation is illustrated in this situation?

Correct answer: c) Business performance.
Market orientation helps a company better understand and respond to customer needs. This can lead to higher profits, increased sales growth and improved market share, which are all indicators of better business performance.

10. A company focuses on developing long-term relationships with customers, suppliers and business partners in order to create ongoing value for all parties involved. Which aspect of holistic marketing is illustrated in this situation?

Correct answer: b) Relationship Marketing.
Relationship marketing focuses on building and maintaining long-term, mutually beneficial relationships with customers, suppliers and business partners. The goal is to create ongoing value and loyalty for all parties.
