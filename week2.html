1. Introduction: scanning the business environment

Business success depends on understanding and adapting to the external environment. Like a ship captain who accounts for currents, winds, and changing weather, a business must scan its environment to identify opportunities and threats. Sustainable growth and market leadership require awareness of customer needs, internal capabilities, competitive forces, and macro-environmental changes.

Central idea: Marketing strategy goes beyond internal operations and direct customer interactions; it also requires continuous analysis of competition and the broader business environment.

The 3 C’s framework

Customer

Understand customer needs, preferences, behaviour, and value.

Company

Assess internal resources, capabilities, strengths, and weaknesses.

Competition

Analyse direct/indirect rivals, substitutes, and the competitive landscape.

Purpose

Match customer needs with company capabilities while responding to competition.

3. Ansoff Matrix

The matrix identifies four growth strategies using existing/new products and existing/new markets.

Product / MarketExisting marketNew market
Existing productMarket Penetration
Sell more existing products in existing markets. Lowest risk		Market Development
Take existing products into new markets. Moderate risk
New productProduct Development
Offer new products in existing markets. Moderate risk		Diversification
New products in entirely new markets. Highest risk
Memory trick: Penetration = same/same; Market Development = same product/new market; Product Development = new product/same market; Diversification = new/new.

Market penetration can include geographic, demographic, or psychographic expansion within an existing market.

4. Treacy and Wiersema’s Value Disciplines

Operational Excellence

Reliable products/services, competitive prices, efficiency, and minimal inconvenience.
Example in notes: IndiGo Airlines.

Product Leadership

Leading-edge products/services that push performance boundaries through innovation and differentiation.
Example in notes: Honda Activa.

Customer Intimacy

Deep relationships, understanding individual needs, and tailored solutions.
Examples in notes: Café Coffee Day and Starbucks.
The chosen discipline should align with the company’s business structure, marketing system, and organizational culture.

5. Marketing objectives and competitive environments

Marketing objectives extend beyond revenue and profit. They can include socially useful exchange, sufficient surplus for survival and growth, and development of a robust marketing organization. Objectives cascade from corporate goals to specific marketing-mix actions.

Strategic lens: 3 C’s (Company, Customers, Competition). Tactical tools: 4 P’s (Product, Price, Place, Promotion).

Market structures

StructureDescription
Pure competitionNumerous sellers in a fragmented market.
OligopolyA few large players dominate the market.
MonopolyOne dominant company.
Monopolistic competitionMany sellers offer similar but differentiated products through branding, features, quality, or design.

Portfolio tools

  • BCG Matrix: Stars, Cash Cows, Dogs, Question Marks. Question Marks have high growth but low market share and often require heavy investment.
  • GE Strategic Planning Grid / GE Matrix: Assesses business units by industry attractiveness and business strength.

6. Porter’s Five Forces

Used to analyse industry attractiveness and competitive intensity.

1. Rivalry

Intensity of competition among existing firms.

2. Supplier power

Suppliers’ influence over price, quality, and availability.

3. New entrants

How easily new firms can enter the industry.

4. Substitutes

Alternative products/services that meet the same need.

5. Buyer power

Customers’ influence over price and terms.
Exam method: Identify each relevant force, explain its effect on competitive pressure or profitability, and apply it to the given situation.

7. Strategy, core competence, and value chain

Following Porter, strategy creates a unique and valuable position through a distinct set of activities.

Three levels of strategy

  • Corporate strategy: Overall mission and resource allocation.
  • Strategic Business Unit (SBU) strategy: Strategy for a specific industry/business sector.
  • Functional strategy: Department-level plans (e.g., marketing or R&D) aligned with corporate and SBU goals.

Porter’s generic strategies

StrategyMeaning
Overall Cost LeadershipBecome the lowest-cost provider; new production technologies can challenge this position.
DifferentiationOffer distinctive features, benefits, or image. Apple is an example in the notes.
FocusServe a narrow customer segment with tailored products. Café Coffee Day is an example in the notes.

Resource-Based View (RBV): VRIN

V — Valuable

Helps exploit opportunities or address threats.

R — Rare

Not widely held by competitors.

I — Inimitable

Difficult or costly to copy.

N — Non-substitutable

Cannot easily be replaced by an equivalent alternative.

Porter’s Value Chain

Primary activities: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing & sales, service.

Support activities: procurement, technology development, human resource management, infrastructure.

Five core business processes

  1. Market sensing
  2. New offering realization
  3. Customer acquisition
  4. Customer relationship management
  5. Fulfillment management

Core competence

Prahalad and Hamel describe it as a capability that contributes significantly to customer-perceived value, can be applied across diverse markets, and is difficult to imitate. Examples include Honda’s engine design and Intel’s microprocessors.

SWOT

Strengths — internal

Advantages such as strong brands, distribution, or expertise.

Weaknesses — internal

Limitations such as resource shortages or limited innovation.

Opportunities — external

Favourable conditions such as demographic shifts or supportive policies.

Threats — external

Unfavourable conditions such as new regulations, rivals, or changing customer preferences.
Important: A strength can become a rigidity—and eventually a threat—if it prevents adaptation. S/W are internal; O/T are external.

9. Competitive analysis and co-opetition

Competition includes direct rivals, indirect rivals, and crossover competitors from other industries. The boundaries between competitors, suppliers, and collaborators may blur; firms may compete in one area and supply one another in another. A competitive landscape map helps visualize these relationships.

Effective analysis continuously evaluates competitor product features, CRM strategies, R&D, and the full marketing mix. It must be integrated with customer understanding.

Barriers to entry

  • Economies of scale: Large-scale operations can lower unit costs.
  • Product differentiation: Unique offerings make it harder for entrants to attract customers.
  • Capital requirements: High start-up investment.
  • Distribution channel control: Established or exclusive access to channels.
  • Switching costs: Inconvenience or expense for customers changing providers.
  • Intellectual property rights: Patents and trademarks provide legal protection.

Co-opetition

Companies can compete and cooperate simultaneously. The notes highlight a shift away from a simplistic zero-sum view and distinguish transparent collaboration from collusive practices.

10. Case study: JC Electrical Contractors Limited

The case concerns an electrical contracting firm in Eastern India. It combines industry analysis, competitive strength, financial indicators, and SWOT to guide strategy.

Five Forces in the case

  • Supplier power: Present, but not explicitly detailed in the notes.
  • Rivalry: Very high because there are numerous competitors.
  • Buyer power: Very high because buyers have many options.
  • New entrants: Low entry barriers, compounded by unlicensed contractors.
  • Substitutes: Non-licensed contractors and technologies such as solar.

Quantitative analysis gave Industry Attractiveness = 69.5 and Competitive Strength = 74. The GE Matrix placed the company in a high-strength quadrant within an attractive industry.

Public-sector competition — “L1, T1”: Lowest Price, Highest Technical Rating. Technical competence is a prerequisite, but price is the ultimate differentiator in the described tender context.

Financial analysis identified free cash flow challenges and a high average collection period, affecting liquidity.

SWOT for JC Electrical

StrengthsWeaknesses
Strong reputation, working relationships, experience curve, revenue growth, core competence in project management software.Not competitive in private-sector price wars, declining net operating profit, high average collection period, high public-to-private business ratio, weakness in medium-voltage applications.
OpportunitiesThreats
Private projects, new sectors/geographies, and services such as home security, medium voltage, and solar.Low entry barriers, rising raw-material prices, and supply shortages.

Strategic priorities

  • Reduce the public-to-private job ratio.
  • Expand geographically.
  • Offer advanced technical services to pursue growth and profitability.

11. Key takeaways

  1. Marketing strategy must consider the wider environment as well as customers, company capabilities, and competition.
  2. The Ansoff Matrix identifies four growth strategies with varying risk.
  3. Value Disciplines are Operational Excellence, Product Leadership, and Customer Intimacy.
  4. Porter’s Five Forces assesses industry competitive pressure.
  5. PESTEL scans macro-environmental factors; SWOT combines internal and external analysis.
  6. VRIN resources and core competencies can support competitive advantage.
  7. Modern competition includes crossover competitors and co-opetition.
  8. Barriers to entry can protect a firm’s market position.
  9. The GE Matrix compares industry attractiveness and competitive strength.

Conclusion: Systematic environmental scanning helps firms anticipate change, identify opportunities, understand threats, and develop strategies for sustainable competitive advantage.

12. Assignment 2 — questions, options, answers & explanations

Questions and options below follow the uploaded Assignment 2 PDF.

Q1. A company introduces a completely new product in a new market where it has not operated before. Which strategy is reflected here?

  1. Market Development
  2. Product Development
  3. Diversification
  4. Penetration
Answer: c) Diversification. Diversification means entering a new market with a completely new product, moving beyond existing products and markets.

Q2. A company designs eco-friendly products, builds a strong marketing team to implement strategies and focuses on earning enough profit to expand its business sustainably. Which marketing objective is MOST directly related to the company’s focus on sustainable profit and growth?

  1. Earning sufficient surplus for survival and growth
  2. Reducing customer satisfaction levels
  3. Performing socially useful exchanges
  4. Developing a marketing organization
Answer: a) Earning sufficient surplus for survival and growth. A business needs sufficient profit to survive, reinvest, expand, and grow sustainably.

Q3. A company focuses on building deep relationships with customers. It understands their specific needs and offers customized solutions rather than standard products. The goal is to provide the best overall solution to each customer. Which value discipline is reflected here?

  1. Cost Leadership
  2. Customer Intimacy
  3. Operational Excellence
  4. Product Leadership
Answer: b) Customer Intimacy. It focuses on understanding individual customer needs and providing personalized solutions.

Q4. In a market, many sellers offer slightly different products (example: branding, features) and each tries to create a unique image to attract customers. Which type of competitive environment is this?

  1. Monopolistic competition
  2. Monopoly
  3. Oligopoly
  4. Pure competition
Answer: a) Monopolistic competition. Many sellers offer similar but differentiated products through branding, features, quality, or design.

Q5. A new company wants to enter an industry but faces high initial investment, strict regulations and strong brand loyalty of existing players. Which competitive force is reflected here?

  1. Rivalry determinants
  2. Buyer power
  3. Supplier power
  4. Entry barriers
Answer: d) Entry barriers. High investment, strict regulations, and customer loyalty make it difficult for new companies to enter.

Q6. A product operates in a high-growth market but has a low market share. It requires heavy investment to grow. Which category does this product belong to in the BCG matrix?

  1. Dog
  2. Star
  3. Question Mark
  4. Cash Cow
Answer: c) Question Mark. It has high market growth but low market share and may need significant investment to increase share.

Q7. A company studies government policies, tax rules and trade regulations before entering a new market. Which factor of the PESTEL framework is being analyzed?

  1. Political
  2. Social
  3. Legal
  4. Economic
Answer: a) Political. The assignment classifies government policies, taxation decisions, and trade regulations under the political environment.

Q8. A company possesses strong brand equity and advanced manufacturing capabilities. However, increasing imports from low-cost international competitors and rapidly changing customer preferences are creating pressure on its market position. In a SWOT analysis, which factor would MOST likely be categorized as a threat?

  1. Strong brand equity
  2. Advanced manufacturing capabilities
  3. Rapidly changing customer preferences
  4. Skilled internal workforce
Answer: c) Rapidly changing customer preferences. A threat is an external factor that can negatively affect performance; changing preferences are outside the company’s direct control.

Q9. A local taxi company starts losing customers not only to other taxi operators in the city but also to app-based food delivery platforms that begin offering passenger transport services. Which type of competitor is BEST represented by the food delivery platforms in this scenario?

  1. Local competitors
  2. Cross-over competitors
  3. Direct competitors
  4. Indirect competitors
Answer: b) Cross-over competitors. Crossover competitors come from another industry and enter or compete in the same market.

Q10. A company operates in a market where many firms offer similar products and new competitors can easily enter the industry. To win government contracts, companies mainly compete by submitting the lowest price quotation. Which basis of competition is MOST evident in this scenario?

  1. Product reputation
  2. Service quality
  3. Brand loyalty
  4. Lowest price bid
Answer: d) Lowest price bid. When firms compete for government contracts mainly by offering the lowest quotation, price becomes the primary basis of competition.
