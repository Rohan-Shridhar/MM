1. Week overview

Understanding customers is only the beginning. A company must turn segmentation and targeting into a practical strategy, then position its offering so that chosen customers see a clear and desirable reason to choose it.

Marketing strategy is connected to broader corporate objectives. It influences resource allocation, product development, the marketing mix, and ultimately market performance. Effective positioning aligns three things:

  • Customer requirements: needs, preferences, and values.
  • Product attributes: the features and benefits the offering can deliver.
  • Company requirements: objectives such as profitability, cash flow, and market share.
Remember: Positioning answers “What should the target customer think of this offering—and why should they choose it?”

2. Marketing strategy: from broad to specific

Strategy operates at connected levels. Broad corporate choices guide narrower decisions until the company defines how an offering should appeal to a selected segment.

LevelWhat it determinesIllustration from the lecture notes
Corporate strategyThe broad scope of the company.Consumer durables.
Business-level strategyThe category or business in which it competes.Home appliances.
Market strategyThe product market and competitive approach.Refrigerators.
Segmentation-based positioning strategyThe chosen customer segment and the offering’s position for that segment.First-time home buyers, replacement buyers, or institutional clients.

Elements of a market-segment strategy

  • Performance objectives: outcomes the company wants to achieve, such as market share, sales, or margins.
  • Strategic focus: the segment and competitive priorities the company chooses to emphasize.
  • Positioning: the distinctive place the offering should occupy in target customers’ minds.
  • Implementation programs: coordinated actions that translate the strategy into practice.

Product life cycle changes priorities

Growth stage

Prioritize growth and a larger market footprint. This may require greater R&D and marketing investment.

Late maturity

Prioritize cash flow, customer retention, micro-segmentation, and profitability from established segments.

Improving sales and margins

Improve sales by…Improve margins by…
Retaining more customers; increasing usage frequency; winning competitors’ customers; entering new market areas.Raising prices; improving sales mix; reducing operating costs; improving asset utilization.

3. Positioning: shaping a mental model

Positioning is a continuous, iterative process that creates a distinct mental model of an offering in the consumer’s mind. It is the strategic foundation for branding and a form of differentiation.

  1. Select target customers. Decide whom the offering is intended to serve.
  2. Analyze competitors. Identify gaps, alternatives, and opportunities.
  3. Design a distinctive value package. Give customers a compelling reason to choose the offering.
  4. Build a web of reasoning. Ensure messages and touchpoints reinforce the intended position.
  5. Make the position tangible. Align the actual offering, communication, and distribution with the desired perception.

Types of positioning appeal

  • Direct and logical: explicitly communicates practical benefits.
  • Subtle and emotional: connects with aspirations, identity, or higher-level values.

The appropriate appeal depends on the product, target audience, and competitive context.

Cemex B2B example: Timeliness, efficiency, specialized equipment, and GPS technology support an overall image of reliability and advanced capability.

Positioning affects the whole organization

Positioning is not only a marketing task. It should guide product design, pricing, promotion, distribution, R&D, supply chain, operations, quality objectives, and finance so that internal decisions support the external image.

Multi-segment positioning: One brand may serve several customer groups. The lecture notes use Lux soap as an example of mass-market, aspirational, and premium segments. This can expand revenue, but may create cannibalization—when products from the same company compete with one another. Cannibalization can be deliberate or unintended.

4. Value proposition

A value proposition summarizes why a customer should buy a product or use a service. It explains the benefits and value offered to the target segment and is closely connected to positioning.

Value typeMeaningExample from Assignment 8
FunctionalPractical performance or utility.Modern workout equipment.
PsychologicalConfidence, identity, feelings, or lifestyle meaning.An elite lifestyle image.
EconomicFinancial benefits or savings.Savings from bundled annual plans.
Exam cue: If a scenario mentions equipment or performance, think functional value; confidence or prestige, psychological value; and savings or price benefits, economic value.

5. Segmentation and targeting in B2B markets

Business-to-business (B2B) marketing sells to organizations rather than individual consumers. Organizational buying often involves multiple people, formal procedures, technical requirements, and longer decision processes.

Ways to segment B2B markets

BasisHow it groups organizations
Demographic characteristicsSales, number of employees, geographic location, or industry (such as petrochemical or pharmaceutical).
Nature of customer activitiesOrganizations that perform similar activities.
End-use applicationHow the purchased product is ultimately used—for example, conveyor belts for automotive versus consumer-product manufacturing.
Purchase characteristics / product typeDifferent requirements based on the role and value of the item being purchased.

B2B product types

TypeDescriptionMarketing emphasis
Entering goodsRaw materials or components that become part of the final product.Reliability, quality, and just-in-time delivery.
Facilitating goodsItems needed for operations but not incorporated into the final product, such as office furniture.Fit with operational needs and dependable supply.
Capital goodsHigh-value equipment purchased infrequently.Technical competence, R&D, and long-term support.

6. Buying center, derived demand, and buying situations

Buying center

A buying center is the group of people in an organization who participate in a purchase decision and share goals or risks. For capital goods, many departments may be involved, making direct sales and personal relationships important. Routine purchases may be handled by a single purchase clerk.

  • Gatekeepers: control or filter access to information, people, or the buying process.
  • Influencers: shape specifications or recommendations without necessarily making the final decision.

Derived demand

B2B demand is often derived from demand for consumer goods and services. For example, demand for components can rise when demand for the final products containing them rises. B2B marketers should therefore monitor end-consumer market trends.

Business buying situations

SituationMeaningMarketing implication
Straight re-buyRoutine reorder.Reliability is especially important.
Modified re-buyA regular purchase with changes or reconsideration.Reputation and quality can matter strongly.
New buyFirst-time purchase, often involving significant expenditure.Provide extensive information and technical support; new suppliers may have an opportunity.

Organizations may also decide whether to make, buy, or lease. Their choice affects supplier opportunities. In international B2B markets, local customs, regulations, cultures, and economic conditions can further shape marketing decisions.

Do not confuse these terms: Derived demand concerns where demand comes from; inelastic demand means quantity demanded changes little when price changes. Assignment 8 Question 5 tests inelastic demand.

7. Crafting positioning: PODs, POPs, and positioning maps

The competitive frame of reference defines the category and alternatives against which customers evaluate an offering.

ConceptMeaningPurpose
Points of Difference (POD)Distinctive attributes or benefits that set an offering apart.Show a reason to prefer the brand. The lecture notes state that PODs should be desirable, deliverable, and differentiable.
Points of Parity (POP)Attributes shared with competitors or expected in the category.Establish category legitimacy and reassure customers that basic expectations are met.

Straddle positioning aims to appeal to two traditionally separate segments. The notes give MUVs as an example.

Positioning maps plot brands according to selected attributes. They help visualize the competitive landscape and identify possible gaps or crowded positions.

Brand narrative and storytelling

Branding communicates the intended position through a compelling story. Strong narratives can appeal to both logical needs and emotional aspirations; brand meaning is understood in relation to the consumer’s life.

8. Competitive strategies by market position

Market positionTypical approachStrategies / emphasis
Market leaderDefend its position while balancing share and profitability.Flank defense, pre-emptive defense, contraction defense; also protect existing customers and expand total market demand.
Market challengerAttack competitors to gain share.Frontal, flanking, encirclement, or guerrilla attacks.
Market followerMaintain share by imitating or slightly improving the leader’s offering.Follow established market approaches rather than directly leading the category.
Niche playerSpecialize in serving a specific segment.Build a foothold by serving a focused group well; particularly useful for startups.

The 40% / 30% / 20% / 10% market-share figures in the lecture notes are illustrations of leader, challenger, follower, and niche positions—not universal thresholds.

10. Last-minute revision

  • Strategy moves from broad corporate choices to business, market, and segment-specific positioning.
  • Positioning balances customer needs, product features, and company objectives.
  • A value proposition explains “why you?” through functional, psychological, and economic value.
  • B2B segmentation considers company characteristics, activities, end use, and product/purchase type.
  • A buying center includes the people involved in organizational buying; gatekeepers and influencers can shape the outcome.
  • Derived demand follows demand in end-consumer markets; inelastic demand responds little to price changes.
  • PODs differentiate; POPs establish category credibility.
  • Leaders defend, challengers attack, followers imitate or improve, and niche players specialize.
  • Positioning should be reflected across the organization—not just in advertising.

11. Assignment 8 — practice MCQs

The following questions and correct options are taken from the uploaded Assignment 8 PDF. Answers are shown for revision.

1. A consumer electronics company expands into the smart home industry, chooses premium innovation and superior technology, then targets urban high-income professionals. Which sequence of strategy levels is reflected?

A. Business Strategy → Market Strategy → Market-Segment Strategy
B. Market Strategy → Business Strategy → Market-Segment Strategy
C. Business Strategy → Market-Segment Strategy → Market Strategy
D. Market-Segment Strategy → Market Strategy → Business Strategy
Answer: A — Business Strategy → Market Strategy → Market-Segment Strategy

2. A smartwatch company targets college students, sets market-share and order-fulfilment goals, focuses on sales volume and Tier-2 students, and offers free study apps to beat a competitor. Which two elements are presented?

A. Positioning and Implementation Programs
B. Performance Objectives and Strategic Focus
C. Strategic Focus and Positioning
D. Implementation Programs and Performance Objectives
Answer: B — Performance Objectives and Strategic Focus

3. A premium fitness club highlights modern equipment, confidence from an elite lifestyle image, and savings through annual plans. Which value combination is represented?

A. Functional, Social and Cultural value
B. Economic, Technological and Emotional value
C. Psychological, Operational and Service value
D. Functional, Psychological and Economic value
Answer: D — Functional, Psychological and Economic value

4. A detergent brand charges different prices in urban and rural markets; dealers resell lower-priced products in higher-priced cities, reducing profits and creating distributor conflict. Which marketing-mix element is most directly involved?

A. Product
B. Pricing
C. Sales force
D. Advertising
Answer: B — Pricing

5. A construction company keeps buying cement and steel despite slight price increases because they are essential to finish a highway project on time. Which demand characteristic is shown?

A. Derived demand
B. Joint demand
C. Inelastic demand
D. Inventory adjustment
Answer: C — Inelastic demand

6. An IT manager’s laptop purchase is delayed by mandatory multi-tier budget review and centralized vendor-compliance procedures. Which influence restricts the choice?

A. Organizational factors
B. Interpersonal influences
C. Environmental factors
D. Individual preferences
Answer: A — Organizational factors

7. A healthcare equipment supplier sells advanced diagnostic machines internationally. Doctors prioritize performance while procurement teams focus on budgets and compliance; local rules, culture, and economic conditions also vary. Which challenge is shown?

A. Organizational buying behavior and domestic reseller issues
B. Consumer decision-making differences and geographic concentration
C. Institutional buying influences and international market complexities
D. Interpersonal conflict in buying centers and standardization challenges
Answer: C — Institutional buying influences and international market complexities

8. A new airline emphasizes comfortable seats, online booking, meals, and safety standards similar to established airlines before promoting unique features. Which positioning concept is conveyed?

A. Market specialization
B. Psychological positioning
C. Points of Difference
D. Points of Parity
Answer: D — Points of Parity

9. A leading soft-drink company encourages consumption during fitness and family occasions, while using loyalty programs and wider distribution to retain customers. Which combination is shown?

A. Market segmentation and niche marketing
B. Increasing market share and concentrated marketing
C. Expanding total market demand and protecting market share
D. Product differentiation and micro marketing
Answer: C — Expanding total market demand and protecting market share

10. A growing smartphone company attacks the industry leader directly by offering similar features at a lower price. Which opponent is targeted?

A. Small local and regional firms
B. The market leader
C. An underfunded firm of the same size
D. The industry as a whole
Answer: B — The market leader
