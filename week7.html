1. Introduction

Modern marketing cannot rely on a one-size-fits-all approach. Customers differ in their needs, preferences, resources, lifestyles, and reasons for buying.

Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning (STP) gives marketers a structured way to identify groups of customers, decide which groups to serve, and create an offering and message that stand out in the minds of those customers.

Examples: McDonald’s adapts menu items to local preferences and cultures. Custom paint services let customers personalize a colour. Both show how understanding customer differences can make an offering more relevant.

2. The STP framework

1. Segmentation

Divide a broad market into meaningful groups with similar needs, benefits sought, values, or characteristics.

Who has similar needs?

2. Targeting

Evaluate the attractiveness of each segment and choose the segment or segments the organization can serve effectively.

Which group should we serve?

3. Positioning

Shape the product and its communication so the target audience understands the distinctive value it offers relative to alternatives.

Why choose us?

How they connect

Segmentation identifies possible groups; targeting chooses the audience; positioning creates a relevant, differentiated value proposition for that audience.

Memory trick: Segment = split the market. Target = select the group. Position = shape the perception.

4. Consumer decision-making models

Waterfall model: the five stages

  1. Need/problem recognition: The consumer notices a gap between the current and desired state.
  2. Information search: The consumer gathers information from memory, people, reviews, advertising, or other sources.
  3. Evaluation of alternatives: The consumer compares brands or solutions using relevant criteria.
  4. Purchase decision: The consumer chooses an option and completes the purchase, subject to situational influences.
  5. Post-purchase evaluation: The consumer compares experience with expectations, influencing satisfaction, loyalty, or regret.
The waterfall is a useful structured model, but real consumers may skip stages, revisit earlier steps, or make impulsive decisions.

ELM versus HEM

ModelWhat influences the choice?ExampleMarketing implication
ELMCareful consideration of relevant information and the strength of arguments (central-route processing).Comparing laptop battery life, processor speed, and warranty.Provide credible evidence, specifications, demonstrations, and clear comparisons.
HEMEmotions, sensory appeal, enjoyment, symbolism, and experience.Choosing a luxury chocolate gift because of its packaging and the pleasure of gifting.Build an appealing experience through design, storytelling, sensory cues, and emotional associations.

Evoked set

The evoked set is the shortlist of brands a consumer remembers when a need arises. Marketers aim to build awareness and positive associations so the brand is part of that shortlist. Strong availability, distinctive positioning, useful reminders, and consistent experiences can help.

5. Market segmentation

Effective segmentation groups customers who have meaningful similarities while distinguishing them from other groups. Segments should be useful for strategy, not just easy to describe.

Common segmentation bases

BasisWhat it considersExample
GeographicCountry, region, city, climate, or population density.Adapting a menu to local food preferences.
DemographicAge, income, household size, occupation, education, or life stage.Products designed for students or families.
PsychographicLifestyle, personality, interests, values, and aspirations.Marketing outdoor equipment to adventure-oriented consumers.
BehaviouralUsage, loyalty, readiness to buy, occasion, or benefits sought.Offering frequent-buyer rewards to loyal customers.
PsychosocialPsychological motives and social identity, including ambitions, values, and lifestyle.Positioning a product around creativity or self-expression.

Criteria for useful market segments

  • Identifiable: The segment and its members can be recognized.
  • Measurable: Its size, purchasing power, and characteristics can be estimated.
  • Accessible: The company can reach and serve it through suitable channels.
  • Substantial / appropriate size: It is large or profitable enough to justify attention.
  • Differentiable: It responds meaningfully differently from other segments.
  • Actionable: The organization can design and implement a suitable strategy for it.

Levels of market segmentation

  • Mass / undifferentiated marketing: One broad offer and marketing mix for the overall market.
  • Segment / differentiated marketing: Different offers or mixes for multiple segments.
  • Niche / concentrated marketing: Focus on a small, specialized group with specific needs.
  • Local marketing: Tailoring offerings to local areas or communities.
  • Individual / customerized marketing: Personalizing elements for individual customers.
A premium skincare line for people with highly sensitive skin is an example of niche marketing: it focuses on a small, specialized group with a distinct need.

6. Targeting, segment scoring, and TAM

After identifying segments, marketers compare their attractiveness with the organization’s objectives, capabilities, and resources.

Scoring segment attractiveness

  1. Identify relevant factors, such as market size, growth, competition, profitability, fit, and risk.
  2. Assign a weight to each factor based on its importance.
  3. Rate each market segment against each factor.
  4. Calculate each factor score by multiplying the factor’s weight by the segment’s rating.
  5. Add the weighted scores and compare segments before making a decision.
Formula: Factor score = Factor weight × Segment rating. The total weighted score helps compare the relative attractiveness of segments.

Total Addressable Market (TAM)

TAM estimates the total revenue opportunity if the business could serve the entire defined market. A clear scope is essential: specify the customer group, geography, product category, and period. TAM is a theoretical opportunity, not a guarantee of actual sales.

VALS-style psychographic profiles

Psychographic frameworks classify consumers by values, motivations, resources, and lifestyle. For example, a confident, well-resourced, innovation-oriented customer who values self-expression, quality, and effectiveness may fit an Actualizer-type profile in the VALS 2 framework. Use the framework’s precise labels when answering course questions.

7. Positioning and perceptual maps

Positioning is the intended place a brand occupies in the target customer’s mind relative to alternatives. Effective positioning gives customers a clear reason to choose the offering.

Elements of a positioning strategy

  • Target audience: Who is the offer designed for?
  • Frame of reference: Which product category or competitive set is relevant?
  • Point of difference: What valuable attribute or benefit makes the offer distinctive?
  • Reason to believe: What evidence supports the promise?

Perceptual maps

A perceptual map plots brands on two attributes customers care about—for example, price on one axis and perceived healthiness on the other. Brands close together are perceived similarly; brands far apart are perceived as different on the selected dimensions.

Illustrative perceptual map

Illustrative only: the points are hypothetical, not real market research data.

Using a perceptual map

  • Identify direct competitors and clusters of similar brands.
  • Look for underserved spaces that might represent opportunities.
  • Check whether the intended brand position matches actual customer perceptions.
  • Use research to validate the chosen attributes and placements.

Customerization

Customerization gives customers some control over product or service features while retaining efficiencies associated with mass production. Examples include selecting a custom paint shade, configuring a laptop, or personalizing a product bundle.

8. Strategic growth planning

Adapted Ansoff Matrix

Existing marketsMarket penetration
Grow sales of current products to current markets.		Product development
Offer new or improved products to current markets.
New marketsMarket development
Take existing products to new customer groups or locations.		Diversification
Enter new markets with new products; often the highest-risk option.

The matrix helps organize growth options by whether the product and market are existing or new. Risk generally increases as the business moves farther from what it already knows, although actual risk depends on the organization and context.

Growth-planning sequence

  1. Set a vision and growth objectives.
  2. Develop potential growth strategies.
  3. Screen opportunities using criteria such as attractiveness, capabilities, resources, and timing.
  4. Select the most suitable opportunity.
  5. Implement the plan and monitor results.

9. Quick revision

  • STP: Segment the market, target selected groups, position the offering in customers’ minds.
  • ELM: Careful attention to message arguments; useful for high-involvement and evidence-driven decisions.
  • HEM: Emotion, sensory appeal, pleasure, and experience.
  • Evoked set: Brands that come to mind when a need arises.
  • Good segments: Identifiable, measurable, accessible, substantial, differentiable, and actionable.
  • Niche marketing: Serves a small, specialized group with particular needs.
  • Perceptual map: Shows how customers perceive brands on selected dimensions.
  • Customerization: Personalization combined with the efficiencies of mass production.
  • TAM: Total theoretical revenue opportunity within a clearly defined market scope.
  • Ansoff Matrix: Market penetration, market development, product development, diversification.
Exam technique: In scenario questions, identify the clue first: rational product comparison → ELM; pleasure or packaging → HEM; price/healthiness chart → perceptual map; small specialist audience → niche marketing; one standard strategy for everyone → undifferentiated marketing.

10. Assignment 7 — MCQs with answers and explanations

Questions and options are reproduced from the supplied Assignment 7 material, with the correct option and explanation shown below each question.

1. A customer carefully compares the battery life, processor speed and warranty of different laptops before making a purchase. Another customer buys a luxury chocolate gift box mainly because of attractive packaging and the pleasure associated with gifting it. Which models of consumer decision-making are BEST reflected in these two situations respectively?

  1. Hedonic Experiential Model and Elaboration Likelihood Model
  2. Elaboration Likelihood Model and Hedonic Experiential Model
  3. Multiattribute Model and Cognitive Dissonance Model
  4. Tricomponent Model and Behavioral Learning Model
Correct answer: b) Elaboration Likelihood Model and Hedonic Experiential Model.
Comparing battery, speed, and warranty involves careful information evaluation, reflecting ELM. Buying chocolate for packaging and pleasure reflects HEM’s emotional and experiential side.

2. A customer enters a supermarket only to buy bread and milk. However, after seeing a “Buy One Get One Free” display near the billing counter, the customer also purchases chocolates and soft drinks that were not originally planned. Which factor affecting consumer purchasing behavior is MOST clearly reflected?

  1. Family life cycle
  2. Social environment
  3. Situational environment
  4. Heredity and home environment
Correct answer: c) Situational environment.
The unexpected purchase is influenced by the in-store situation and promotion rather than the customer’s original plan.

3. A company planning to enter the electric vehicle market first defines its long-term vision and growth objectives. It then evaluates market attractiveness, resource availability and timing before finally launching the product in selected cities. Which sequence of strategic growth planning is BEST reflected?

  1. Strategy for Growth → Screening Criteria → Implementation
  2. Screening Criteria → Strategy for Growth → Product Development
  3. Vision → Implementation → Screening Criteria
  4. Implementation → Screening Criteria → Strategy for Growth
Correct answer: a) Strategy for Growth → Screening Criteria → Implementation.
The company determines its growth strategy, evaluates opportunities using screening criteria, and then implements the selected plan.

4. A consumer spends most of their free time attending technology workshops, reading books on innovation and exploring new software tools. The person prefers investing in learning and knowledge-building activities rather than entertainment or luxury products. According to Starch’s Six Global Consumer Segments, which segment does this consumer MOST likely belong to?

  1. Devouts
  2. Fun Seekers
  3. Strivers
  4. Creatives
Correct answer: d) Creatives.
Creatives value learning, knowledge, innovation, and intellectual or creative activities more than luxury or entertainment.

5. A company divides the market into different customer groups based on lifestyle and income. One selected segment is large enough to be profitable, can be clearly identified and can be reached effectively through digital advertising campaigns. Which criteria of a good market segment are MOST clearly reflected?

  1. Measurable, unstable and undifferentiated
  2. Stable, measurable and differentiated
  3. Accessible, identifiable and appropriate size
  4. Differentiated, unstable and inaccessible
Correct answer: c) Accessible, identifiable and appropriate size.
The segment can be identified, is large enough to be profitable, and can be reached through digital advertising.

6. A company is evaluating three different market segments before launching a new product. Management identifies important factors such as growth potential and profitability, assigns weights to each factor, rates each segment and calculates total scores. Which step involves multiplying the factor weight by the segment rating?

  1. Develop factor scores
  2. Identify factors
  3. Weight factors
  4. Rate market segments
Correct answer: a) Develop factor scores.
The factor score is calculated by multiplying the importance weight by the segment’s rating.

7. A beverage company creates a visual chart comparing different soft drink brands based on “price” and “healthiness.” Brands positioned close together are perceived similarly, while brands placed far apart are viewed differently. Which concept is BEST illustrated?

  1. Market segmentation
  2. Perceptual map
  3. Product life cycle
  4. Conjoint analysis
Correct answer: b) Perceptual map.
A perceptual map visually represents consumer perceptions of brands based on attributes such as price and healthiness.

8. A company launches a premium organic skincare line designed for consumers with highly sensitive skin. The products target a small, specialized group with unique needs rather than the mass market. Which level of market segmentation does this represent?

  1. 1:2:1 Marketing
  2. Segment Marketing
  3. Localised Marketing
  4. Niche Marketing
Correct answer: d) Niche Marketing.
Niche marketing focuses on a small, specialized customer group with specific needs.

9. A marketing manager is launching a premium electric scooter priced at ₹3 lakhs, designed for environmentally conscious, tech-savvy and style-oriented urban professionals who value self-expression and achievement. The target customers are confident, have abundant resources, lead diverse lives and are open to new innovations. They are not motivated primarily by status or peer approval but by their own sense of aesthetics and effectiveness. According to the VALS 2 framework, which segment should the manager target most?

  1. Achievers
  2. Experiencers
  3. Actualizers
  4. Fulfilled
Correct answer: c) Actualizers.
Actualizers are confident, well-resourced consumers who value self-expression, innovation, quality, and effectiveness rather than mainly seeking status or peer approval.

10. A multinational beverage company sells the same soft drink across the country using a single advertising campaign, uniform packaging and one standard marketing strategy for all customers without targeting any specific segment. Which strategy for reaching the target market does this represent?

  1. Differentiated Marketing
  2. Undifferentiated Marketing
  3. Concentrated Marketing
  4. Micro Marketing
Correct answer: b) Undifferentiated Marketing.
Undifferentiated marketing uses one standard strategy for the entire market rather than addressing segments separately.
